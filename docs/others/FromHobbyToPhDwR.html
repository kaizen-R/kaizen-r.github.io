Interlude: What is an “LCS”?

In the 80’s, “AI” was approached as “Expert Systems”: Humans would write rules to define some behaviour. A difficulty was how to come up with the best rules a good model would need. ~1976, John H. Holland proposed the “Learning Classifier System”, leveraging his Genetic Algorithms.


A “model”, a population of rules could look a bit like this:

Rule Interpretation
10## -> X if A & NOT(B) (and C and D don’t matter) then Class=X
###1 -> Y if D then Class=Y

An LCS tries to evolve the best possible rules using stochastic processes (i.e. the result is not “the” guaranteed best), using a mix of covering, genetic crossing & mutation, and selection with pressure towards general but accurate rules. This is automated search of solutions, “ML”.


An LCS model is traditionally a population of “human-readable” rules, which is good for mitigating bias(es) (in training data, at least) and increased trust (justifying decisions).

Variants of LCS have been applied to Reinforcement (notably XCS) and Supervised Learning (e.g. UCS).

And why a PhD?

At these very conferences: “AI could write my thesis”. Well, “AI” can write papers, indeed! But why do a PhD then?


Naive (and idealistic, and romantic) take:

It is about becoming an expert through thinking & making some scientific progress (even something minuscule). And maybe it’s one way to direct learning efforts.


Also I now get excellent feedback from experts (my Thesis Supervisors). I’m not alone anymore!


And my wife wouldn’t let me study another MSc. 😅

I jumped in my seat, 2 years ago, with these declaration, AI can write my thesis. Of course, but then why are we here?

Today I value the setting of clear directions, the push, and above all super clever people to have interesting conversations with on the topic that somehow currently keeps me in love with computing, ML etc.

I KNOW, I realize I am naive and idealistic and romantic here. A PhD is supposed to be about making a scientific progress, something not existing yet. Critically, I also understand that maybe a speck of dust in the grand scheme of things, something tiny, minor, minuscule. But something new, nevertheless. A new conclusion, a new approach, a never documented before combination of techniques.

I am fortunate enough that I don’t need to do a PhD. I am not an academic, barely an engineer at this point. Nobody requires it from me, I won’t get a promotion for it (3-5 years from now when I’m done…) I just like to learn and understand things. I love the idea of coming up with something that will be a bit better. A grain of sand at best, but I will make the smallest of difference.

Paradox?


I have a critical stance on the (incorrect) use of LLMs. Yet I study Machine Learning?


Yes. I think LLMs and agentic AI is extremly powerful. But I believe that they are over-used, for the wrong reasons, given the (mentioned) issues.


One big focus for me is how to use the machine to help me make choices/decisions.

  • Deep Learning is (mostly) a black box. LLMs make the reasoning and decisions for us.

  • I want ML and computers to help us. I want eXplainable/Interpretable AI. I chose the LCS algorithm in big part because of that, while I realize it’s no competition for a 200+ billion parameters transformer…

  • We shouldn’t aim to replace ourselves, but to improve & elevate ourselves.

Use Agentic AI to make important progress. Fight cancer, climate change. Of course! Go right ahead.

But given the trade-offs, maybe hold off on generating “AI Slop” videos?

Did I use LLMs then?

  • Not for any code of RLCS. I didn’t have a time pressure. Coding is a satisfying experience.

  • I did not use them that much for learning…

    • I prioritized books. And (later) papers.

    • Which possibly was a mistake (as long as you check the sources)

Maybe not using AI is really a privilege?

  • Do I use them? Yes (more at work, day-to-day, for coding at times, but I focus mostly on understanding how to use agentic GenAI securely)
  • Here, mostly to help me find literature (semantic understanding is super helpful!)
  • and indeed, sometimes, to discuss my own ideas, as a “sparring opponent” (being aware the vendor will have access and train on such conversations)

Note: Ensure Authorship is clear. It’s a tool, yes, and you don’t need to mention Excel. But the code you push with an Agent, is not your thinking alone. So you’re not the (only) author of it.

Conclusions

It was never about the syntax. It was (and still is) about thinking through (programmatic) approaches to solving problems. And coding in R is just as good an option as any other for that.


We should still read books. We should still code (even if only from time to time and for fun). We should still make time to think, get bored. Maybe not at our day job, “because competition”. But this is precisely why we must make an effort to reserve space for our own “mental gym”.


But isn’t learning fun in-and-of-itself?


Taking our time to learn and understand is becoming a luxury.
